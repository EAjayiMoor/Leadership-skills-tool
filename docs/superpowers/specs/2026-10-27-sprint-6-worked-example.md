## Question and evidence

Synthetic question Q2: “How do leaders make responsibilities clear when decisions cross organisational boundaries?” The demo assigns records to the role groups captured in the profile. Role labels below are therefore placeholders for the selected profile's first and second role groups.

| Source | Synthetic passage | What it can support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q2-S1, survey, P01, first selected role group | “We discuss decision clarity at shared planning meetings about research, manufacturing and commercial handoffs. We can usually explain the priorities we are working towards.” | Shared planning and clear priorities are present in this example. It cautions against claiming that cross-functional coordination is uniformly unclear. |
| Q2-S2, survey, P02, second selected role group | “In some decisions involving research, manufacturing and commercial handoffs, I still have to ask who owns the next step. The shared meetings do not resolve every exception.” | Some exceptions leave next-step ownership unclear in this example. It does not establish how often this occurs. |
| Q2-I1, interview, P01, first selected role group | “A recent discussion about research, manufacturing and commercial handoffs helped us agree a next step. I would find it useful to record who follows through, especially when priorities change.” | Mixed evidence: one discussion resolved a next step, while the speaker still wants clearer follow-through when priorities change. |

P01 appears in both Q2-S1 and Q2-I1. These are three fictional passages from two participant codes, not three independent confirmations. There is only one survey passage per selected role group and no repeated sample from which to infer a role-level pattern.

## Candidate finding

> In these fictional examples, shared planning helps make priorities clear and can help agree next steps. Ownership of exceptions and follow-through when priorities change may still need to be made more explicit.

**Trace and balance:** Q2-S1 supports the first sentence. Q2-I1 also describes a next step being agreed. Q2-S2 supports the concern about exceptions; Q2-I1 qualifies it by showing a successful discussion while also asking for follow-through to be recorded.

**Limits:** This is a cautious interpretation of prepared passages, not a claim about prevalence, cause, overall leadership capability, or differences between role groups. The examples do not establish that the handoff problem is widespread or that a process change will solve it. The passages are fictional illustrations, not answers to the consultant's edited question.

## Linked candidate recommendation

**Action:** Trial a lightweight handoff note for decisions spanning research, manufacturing and commercial teams. Record the agreed owner, next step and a follow-up point when priorities change.

**Rationale:** Q2-S2 describes uncertainty over who owns the next step in some exceptions. Q2-I1 asks for follow-through to be recorded. Q2-S1 suggests shared planning is useful, so the proposal tests a way to carry decisions through those meetings rather than assuming the meetings themselves should stop.

**Intended outcome:** Make ownership and follow-through visible for cross-functional decisions. After a period agreed with the sponsor, review a small sample of handoffs for a named owner, next step and follow-up. No success threshold is assumed here.

This is a proposal for consultant review, not an instruction to the client. It should only flow into a report if its linked finding is accepted and the recommendation is separately reviewed.

## Review prompts

3. Is it clear that the examples cannot establish prevalence or a difference between role groups?
4. Is the proposed action proportionate to the finding, and is its intended outcome clear?
